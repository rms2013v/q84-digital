From spreadsheets to Business Central

When inventory, pricing and orders live in Excel, nobody trusts the numbers. We turn your spreadsheets into clean Business Central data.

The problem

Why spreadsheets stop working

  • Several versions of the truth. Each team keeps its own copy, and they never match.
  • No audit trail. Nobody knows who changed a price or a stock level, or when.
  • Manual re-entry. Orders and invoices are typed again into the accounting system.
  • Errors that cost money. A wrong formula becomes a wrong order or a wrong margin.

In depth

What changes when you stop running on spreadsheets

Moving off spreadsheets isn’t only a data load. It changes who owns each piece of information and how changes are controlled.

One owner per record

In Business Central, each item, customer, vendor and price has a single record. Instead of several people keeping their own copies, specific roles are allowed to create and change each type of record, and every change is logged. That alone removes most of the “which version is right?” arguments.

Item data that works for everyone

The item list is usually the hardest part. A clean item record needs a consistent number, a clear description, a base unit of measure plus purchase and sales units, a category, a costing method, and the posting group that sends it to the right accounts. Getting those right once means sales, purchasing, warehouse and finance all work from the same data.

Excel stays, in a better role

Your team doesn’t have to give up Excel. Business Central opens almost any list in Excel, and many lists can be edited there and published back. The difference is that Excel becomes a view of the real data rather than the place the data lives.

Start small

If the spreadsheets are very messy, it can help to go live with finance, customers, vendors and items first, and bring in warehouse bins and detailed pricing in a second phase once the basics are stable.

Typical spreadsheets we replace

  • Item and price lists become items, units of measure and price lists.
  • Stock counts by location become inventory by location and bin, updated by every transaction.
  • Purchase trackers become purchase orders with expected receipt dates.
  • Order logs become sales orders linked to shipments and invoices.
  • Customer lists with credit notes become customers with credit limits and payment terms.

Signs it’s time to move off spreadsheets

  • More than one person updates the same spreadsheet each day.
  • Stock levels in the spreadsheet regularly disagree with the shelf.
  • Orders, invoices or purchase orders are typed into more than one place.
  • Month-end depends on someone copying spreadsheet totals into the accounts.
  • Only one person really understands how the spreadsheets fit together.

If two or more apply, the time spent maintaining spreadsheets is likely already more than an ERP would cost.

After go-live

Retire the old spreadsheets deliberately. Archive them read-only on go-live day so nobody keeps updating them by habit, and point every team to the Business Central list or Excel view that replaces each one. In the first weeks, the most common question is “where do I find what I used to look up in the spreadsheet?”, so a short list mapping old files to new screens saves a lot of time.

FAQ

Spreadsheet migration questions

Have a different question? Ask directly.

Do we need to clean our spreadsheets before starting?

No. Send them as they are. We find duplicates, gaps and inconsistent codes and give you a short list of business decisions, such as which item numbers to keep.

Can we still use Excel with Business Central?

Yes. Business Central opens almost any list in Excel, and many lists can be edited in Excel and published back. Your team keeps the tool they know, with one source of truth behind it.

How do we get accurate opening stock?

With a physical count just before go-live. We load item data in advance, then post the counted quantities as opening stock at cutover.
