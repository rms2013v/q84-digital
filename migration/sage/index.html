Moving from Sage 50 or Sage 100? We map your Sage data and modules to Business Central, clean it up and reconcile every balance before you switch.

Why companies move

When Sage stops keeping up

  • Sage 50 limits. Inventory, multi-location stock and users outgrow a small-business system.
  • Sage 100 on old servers. On-premises hardware, add-ons and upgrades to maintain.
  • Disconnected tools. E-commerce, warehouse and reporting running beside Sage, not inside it.

In depth

Planning a Sage to Business Central migration

Sage 50 or Sage 100 makes a difference

Sage 50 companies are usually moving because they’ve outgrown a small-business accounting system: they need proper inventory, more users, multiple locations or better reporting. The migration looks much like a QuickBooks move: master data, open items and balances, with stock coming from a count.

Sage 100 companies often run a full ERP already, with sales orders, purchasing, inventory and sometimes bills of materials and work orders, on their own servers with add-ons. Their move is closer to an ERP replacement: each module is mapped to Business Central, add-ons are reviewed, and open orders are migrated as well as balances.

How we get data out of Sage

There’s no Microsoft migration tool for Sage, so we extract data through Sage’s own reports and exports and, where available, its database connection. Everything is staged in templates, cleaned, and loaded into a Business Central test company with configuration packages. Trial loads are reconciled against Sage’s trial balance and aging reports before the final cutover.

Common differences to plan for

  • Sub-accounts and departments in the Sage chart of accounts usually become dimensions.
  • Price levels and customer-specific pricing move to Business Central price lists, which are more flexible.
  • Costing methods must be confirmed per item before stock is loaded.
  • Custom reports and Crystal reports are rebuilt only where people still use them.

Running Sage and Business Central in parallel

Most companies don’t run both systems side by side for long; it doubles the data entry. Instead, Sage stays available for lookups after go-live, and the first Business Central month-end is reconciled closely against expected results. If your auditor or lender requires it, a parallel close for one period can be planned into the timeline.

Before you start

  • Run and save your trial balance, A/R and A/P aging, and inventory valuation for the last closed month.
  • Make customers, vendors and items you no longer use inactive.
  • List the Sage add-ons, custom reports and integrations you rely on.
  • Agree a cutover date with your accountant, ideally a month or quarter end.

With those in hand, the first trial load can usually happen within a few weeks of the project starting.

After go-live

The first month-end in Business Central is the real test. We reconcile it with your finance team line by line against what Sage would have shown, and adjust any posting setup that doesn’t match how you report. From the second month, the close is usually faster than it was in Sage, because inventory, receivables and payables all post straight to the general ledger.

FAQ

Sage migration questions

Have a different question? Ask directly.

Does Microsoft provide a migration tool for Sage?

Not a dedicated one like it does for Dynamics GP. We extract data from Sage using its reports and, where available, its ODBC connection, then load it with Business Central’s configuration packages.

Can Business Central replace Sage 100 manufacturing?

For most discrete manufacturers, yes. Business Central Premium covers bills of materials, production orders, routings and costing. We compare your Sage 100 work order setup with Business Central during discovery. Business Central for manufacturing →

How long does a Sage migration take?

The data migration usually runs over three to six weeks alongside the wider implementation, with at least two trial loads before cutover. Implementation timeline →
