Dynamics NAV to Business Central upgrade

Business Central is the modern version of NAV. Getting there depends on your NAV version and how much custom code you carry. We’ll help you choose the right path.

Two paths

Upgrade or re-implement?

There’s no single right answer. The choice depends on your version, your customizations and how well NAV still fits your business.

Technical upgrade

Bring your data and setup forward

  • Best for recent NAV versions with clean data
  • Keeps your history and setup largely as-is
  • Customizations rewritten as AL extensions
  • Several upgrade steps for older versions
Re-implementation

Start clean, bring the data you need

  • Best for older versions or heavy customization
  • Uses standard Business Central features first
  • Open items, balances and summary history migrated
  • Often simpler to maintain afterwards

What it costs

What a NAV move usually costs

Most NAV moves are mid-sized projects, because years of custom code have to be replaced, rebuilt or retired. The price depends far more on that code than on your number of users.

  • Custom code. The single biggest driver. An object analysis shows what can be retired or replaced with standard features.
  • Your NAV version. Older versions need more upgrade steps, so they often get a fresh implementation instead.
  • Add-ons and integrations. Each needs a Business Central version, an app or a rebuild.
  • History. Open items and summary history keep the project lean; NAV stays read-only for lookups.
Custom code

What happens to your NAV customizations

Years of NAV customizations are often the biggest part of the project. Many are no longer needed because Business Central now does the job as standard.

  • Replace with standard. Features that Business Central has added since your NAV version.
  • Replace with an app. Proven AppSource apps for EDI, warehouse scanning, shipping and more.
  • Rewrite as an extension. Genuinely unique processes rebuilt in AL, upgrade-safe.
  • Retire. Reports and fields nobody uses anymore.

In depth

How NAV and Business Central differ

Business Central grew out of Dynamics NAV, so finance and operations users will recognize much of it: the same document flow, posting groups and journals. The bigger differences are underneath.

  • Cloud first. Business Central online is hosted and updated by Microsoft. No servers, SQL maintenance or upgrade projects every few years.
  • Extensions instead of modified code. NAV customizations changed Microsoft’s code directly, which made every upgrade a project. Business Central customizations are extensions written in AL that sit beside the standard code.
  • Web and mobile clients. Business Central runs in a browser and on phones and tablets, with no Windows client to install.
  • Microsoft 365 integration with Outlook, Excel, Teams and Power BI.

Which NAV version you’re on matters

Recent NAV versions can usually be upgraded with their data and setup, through a Business Central on-premises version and then Microsoft’s cloud migration. Older versions, or systems with heavy customization, often move faster and cleaner with a fresh implementation that brings over master data, open items and summary history. We recommend a path after looking at your version, objects and add-ons.

Before you start

List every customization and add-on, note which department relies on each one, and identify the reports people actually run. That list drives most of the cost and is the first thing we review in an assessment.

On-premises or online?

Business Central online removes servers, backups and upgrade projects, and is the right choice for most companies. On-premises Business Central still exists for specific needs, such as strict data residency or heavy local integrations, but it brings back the upgrade and hosting work that moving off NAV usually aims to remove.

Your NAV data, setup knowledge and process experience aren’t wasted. Most of it carries straight into Business Central; what changes is the technology underneath.

We start every NAV project with a short assessment of your version, objects and add-ons, and give you a written recommendation on the path before any larger commitment.

FAQ

NAV upgrade questions

Have a different question? Ask directly.

Can we upgrade NAV directly to Business Central online?

Not in one step. NAV 2018 and earlier versions first need upgrading to a Business Central on-premises version, then Microsoft’s cloud migration moves the data online. Older NAV versions often get a fresh implementation with data migration instead, which can be quicker and cleaner.

What happens to our NAV customizations?

Old C/AL code can’t run in Business Central online. Each customization is either replaced with a standard feature, replaced with an AppSource app, or rewritten as an AL extension. Many turn out to be unnecessary. AL development →

Is NAV still supported?

Every version of Dynamics NAV is past mainstream support. NAV 2018, the last version, reaches the end of extended support on January 11, 2028. Check Microsoft’s lifecycle pages for your exact version.

How much does moving from NAV to Business Central cost?

Most NAV moves are mid-sized projects, because the custom code has to be replaced, rebuilt or retired. The biggest cost drivers are your NAV version, the amount of custom code, add-ons, integrations and how much history you bring. We give a fixed quote after a short assessment. NAV to Business Central cost guide →

Should we move to the cloud or stay on-premises?

Most companies choose Business Central online: Microsoft handles hosting, updates and backups. On-premises still exists for specific needs, and we’ll discuss the trade-offs with you.
