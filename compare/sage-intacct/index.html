Business Central vs Sage Intacct

Sage Intacct is a finance-first cloud accounting system. Business Central is a full ERP. The right choice depends on how much of your business is inventory and operations. Updated November 21, 2026 · Q84 Digital consultants

Inventory-heavy or finance-first?

The most useful question is where your complexity sits. If the hard part of your business is purchasing, stock, warehouse and fulfillment, you need a full ERP, and Business Central handles those processes natively. If the hard part is accounting itself, such as many entities, complex revenue recognition or detailed dimensional reporting with little or no inventory, a finance-first system like Sage Intacct is built for that.

Multi-entity and consolidation

Both handle several companies. Sage Intacct is known for multi-entity accounting and dimension-based reporting. Business Central runs each entity as a company with consolidation and inter-company postings, which suits most small and midsize groups, and Power BI can report across companies when you need a combined view.

What it means for your team

With a finance-first system, operations teams often keep separate tools for inventory, orders or projects, and those need integrating. With a full ERP, more of the business works in one system, but setup covers more departments. Be clear about which teams the new system has to serve before comparing products.

Choose Business Central if

Business Central fits better when…

  • You hold inventory, run a warehouse or manufacture products.
  • You want one system for operations and finance, not finance plus separate tools.
  • Your team lives in Microsoft 365.
Choose Sage Intacct if

Sage Intacct fits better when…

  • You’re a services, nonprofit or software company with little or no inventory.
  • Finance is the main buyer and multi-entity accounting is the core need.
  • You already run Sage products and want to stay in that family.

We implement Business Central, so we’re not neutral. We’ve tried to be fair, and we’ll tell you on a call if another system suits you better.

In depth

Questions to settle before choosing between Business Central and Sage Intacct

Which teams must the system serve?

List every team that will use the new system daily. If it’s mainly finance and leadership, a finance-first system can be the cleaner choice. If purchasing, warehouse, production or project teams also need it, a full ERP avoids building and maintaining integrations between separate tools.

How do you report today, and how do you want to?

Both systems support dimensional reporting. Write down the reports your CFO and board actually use and ask each vendor to show how they’re produced, including how much setup they need and whether they can be changed without a consultant.

What will you integrate?

Count the systems that would need to connect: CRM, e-commerce, warehouse, payroll, banking. Each integration has a build cost and an upkeep cost. A system that covers more of the business natively needs fewer of them.

Five-year cost for the same scope

Compare licenses, implementation, integrations and support over five years, for the same users and processes. A finance-first system plus separate operational tools can cost more in total than one ERP, or less, depending on how much of your business is operational.

Growth over the next five years

Consider where the business is heading. Adding inventory, a warehouse or light manufacturing later is straightforward in a full ERP; in a finance-first system it usually means adding and integrating another product.

The honest summary

For services, nonprofit or software companies with little inventory and a strong focus on multi-entity accounting, Sage Intacct is a strong, focused choice. For companies that buy, stock, make or ship physical goods, or expect to soon, Business Central covers operations and finance in one system and is usually the better long-term fit.

Whichever you lean towards, ask for a demo of your own month-end and one operational process, and talk to a reference customer of a similar size in your industry.

A note on Sage’s other products

Sage sells several different accounting and ERP products, including Sage 50, Sage 100, Sage 300 and Sage Intacct. They are separate systems rather than editions of one product, so moving between them is a new implementation, just like moving to Business Central. If you’re on Sage 50 or Sage 100 today, compare Sage Intacct and Business Central on equal terms: both mean new software, new processes and a data migration. See moving from Sage to Business Central for what that involves.

A short paid discovery with each shortlisted vendor, focused on your hardest processes, is often the best money you spend in the whole selection.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Have a different question? Ask directly.

Can Sage Intacct handle inventory?

It has an inventory module that suits simpler needs. Companies with warehouses, bins, picking or manufacturing usually find a full ERP such as Business Central a better fit.

We use Sage 50 or Sage 100. Is Intacct the natural next step?

Not automatically. Sage Intacct is a different product from Sage 50 and Sage 100, so either move is a new implementation. Choose on fit, not on the name. Sage to Business Central →
