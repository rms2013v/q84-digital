Business Central vs NetSuite

Both are leading cloud ERPs for growing companies. They differ most in pricing model, ecosystem and how they’re customized. Updated November 21, 2026 · Q84 Digital consultants

Side by side

Comparing cost fairly

Business Central publishes its per-user prices: Essentials and Premium for full users, and low-cost Team Members licenses for people who mostly view and approve. NetSuite is quoted per customer, usually as a base platform fee plus users and add-on modules. That makes a like-for-like comparison harder than it looks.

The fairest way to compare is to price the same scope on both: the same number of full and light users, the same modules, and three to five years of subscription rather than year one. Ask both vendors how renewal pricing works, since multi-year contract terms make a real difference to total cost.

Who implements it

Business Central is sold and implemented almost entirely through Microsoft partners, so you can choose a firm by industry, size and location, and change partners later without changing software. NetSuite can be implemented by Oracle NetSuite’s own services team or by partners. Either way, the consultant’s experience in your industry matters more than the product logo.

Day to day with Microsoft 365

For teams that live in Outlook, Excel and Teams, Business Central’s Microsoft 365 integration is a practical difference. Sales staff can create quotes from Outlook, finance can open almost any list in Excel and publish changes back, and colleagues can share a live customer or order card in a Teams chat.

Switching between them

Companies move in both directions. A move is a new implementation either way: master data, open transactions and balances are migrated, and detailed history usually stays in the old system for reference.

Choose Business Central if

Business Central fits better when…

  • Your company runs on Microsoft 365 and wants Outlook, Excel and Teams to work with the ERP.
  • You want published per-user pricing and a choice of implementation partners.
  • You’re moving from QuickBooks, Dynamics GP or Dynamics NAV.
  • Inventory, warehousing and light manufacturing are central to the business.
Choose NetSuite if

NetSuite fits better when…

  • You run many subsidiaries across countries and need deep global consolidation from day one.
  • You prefer a single vendor for software and implementation.
  • You already use NetSuite add-ons or SuiteCommerce heavily.

We implement Business Central, so we’re not neutral. We’ve tried to be fair, and we’ll tell you on a call if another system suits you better.

In depth

Questions to settle before choosing between Business Central and NetSuite

How many entities, and where?

For one company or a few US entities, both systems handle consolidation well. For many subsidiaries across countries and currencies, look closely at how each handles inter-company transactions, local tax and statutory reporting, and whether your partner has done it before.

How much of the day happens in Microsoft 365?

If your team lives in Outlook, Excel and Teams, Business Central’s built-in integration saves time every day. If you use Google Workspace or other tools, that advantage matters less.

Who will support you after go-live?

Ask both vendors who handles day-to-day support, what it costs, and how quickly typical requests are resolved. The quality of your support partner often matters more to long-term satisfaction than the product itself.

What does the five-year cost look like?

Ask for a five-year view that includes licenses, renewals, apps, implementation and support for the same scope. Year-one quotes can look similar while year-five costs differ a lot.

Can you see your own processes in a demo?

Ask each vendor to demo three of your real processes, such as a customer order with a backorder, a purchase with landed cost, and month-end, rather than a standard script. How each system handles your actual work is the best comparison you can get.

Migration and switching costs

Whichever system you choose, plan the move from your current one carefully. The data migration, the processes your team has to relearn and the integrations you rebuild usually cost more than the difference in license prices. Choosing the system your team can adopt quickly, with a partner who has done your kind of migration before, matters more than small differences in features.

The honest summary

For a US company with one or a few entities, inventory to manage and a team that works in Microsoft 365, Business Central is usually the more cost-effective choice. For a fast-growing company with many international subsidiaries that wants one vendor for software and services, NetSuite is often worth its higher price.

If you’re still unsure, list your ten most important requirements and score both systems against them with your team, rather than relying on feature lists.

Both are capable systems; the right one is the one that fits how your business runs.
