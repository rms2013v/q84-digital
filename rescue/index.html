Business Central rescue and recovery

Your Business Central project has stalled, run over budget, or gone live without working properly. We find out why and get it back on track.

Warning signs

Does this sound familiar?

A struggling ERP rarely fails all at once. It shows up as workarounds, spreadsheets coming back and a team that stops trusting the numbers.

  • The go-live date keeps moving. Months later, you’re still not live and the budget is gone.
  • Live, but nobody trusts it. Inventory, receivables or margins don’t match reality.
  • Month-end takes longer than before. Finance reconciles Business Central against spreadsheets.
  • Your partner has gone quiet. Tickets sit unanswered, or the consultants you knew have left.
  • Too much custom code. Every Microsoft update breaks something.

Root causes

What usually goes wrong

Most rescues trace back to the same handful of causes. Knowing which one you have tells us how big the fix is.

  • Posting setup. General and inventory posting groups that don’t match how the business reports.
  • Data loaded as-is. Duplicates, wrong units of measure and bad opening balances.
  • Processes never finished. Warehouse, approvals or purchasing set up halfway.
  • Customization instead of configuration. Code replacing features Business Central already has.
  • No training. Users never learned the new way, so they kept the old one.
Worked example · Illustrative scenario

Fixing month-end at a distributor whose project stalled

A 30-person distributor went live on Business Central with its first partner a year earlier. Inventory value never matches the balance sheet, month-end takes two weeks, and the original consultants have moved on.

StageWhat happens
Week 1Triage: fix the two integrations that stopped syncing orders, so daily trading is back to normal.
Weeks 2 to 3Health check finds inventory posting groups mapped to the wrong accounts and item costs never adjusted.
Weeks 4 to 6Correct posting setup, run cost adjustment, reconcile inventory to the general ledger and document the month-end routine.
Weeks 7 to 8Replace a custom costing report with standard Business Central reports and train the finance team on the new close checklist.

Outcome: Inventory reconciles to the balance sheet every month, and month-end drops from two weeks to three days.

This example shows how we approach this kind of project. It is not a client case study.

In depth

Rescue: what to gather before your first call

A recovery moves faster when we can see the system and the history quickly. If you can, collect the following before the health check starts:

  • Admin access to your Business Central environments (production and any sandboxes).
  • The original scope, solution design or statement of work, even if it’s out of date.
  • A list of installed extensions and apps, and who built each custom one.
  • Your top five problems in your team’s own words, with an example document for each.
  • The last two month-end close checklists or reconciliations.

None of this has to be perfect. Missing documentation is common in rescue projects, and rebuilding it is part of the work.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Have a different question? Ask directly.

Can we switch Business Central partners in the middle of a project?

Yes. Your Business Central environment and data belong to you, and your licenses can stay where they are or move to a new partner. We’ll help you get admin access, documentation and any custom code handed over cleanly.

Do we have to start over?

Rarely. Most struggling systems have a sound foundation with specific problems on top: wrong setup, bad data or unfinished processes. We fix those first. A fresh start is only worth it when the setup is wrong at its core, and we’ll show you why before recommending it.

How quickly can you start?

The health check usually starts within one to two weeks of your call. If something is blocking daily work, such as invoices that won’t post, we deal with that first.

What does a health check cost?

It’s a fixed-price review agreed up front, sized to your system. You get a written report you can act on with us, on your own or with another partner.
