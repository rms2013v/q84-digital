Business Central for e-commerce brands

Orders from every channel in one system, stock you can trust across warehouses and 3PLs, and payouts that reconcile without a spreadsheet.

Common challenges

Where growing online brands get stuck

  • Overselling. Stock in the store doesn’t match the warehouse.
  • Orders typed twice. Web orders re-entered for fulfillment and accounting.
  • Payouts that don’t reconcile. Fees, refunds and reserves make bank matching painful.
  • Margins you can’t see. Shipping, returns and channel fees never reach product margin.

In depth

What e-commerce brands get from moving to an ERP

Most online brands start with their store platform plus accounting software, and add tools as they grow: an inventory app, a shipping tool, spreadsheets for purchasing. Somewhere past a few thousand orders a month, the gaps between those tools become the biggest cost in the business.

One stock position across every channel

Business Central becomes the single place that knows what’s in each warehouse or 3PL and what’s already promised to open orders. It tells each channel what is available to sell, so a busy weekend on one channel doesn’t oversell stock another channel already sold.

Purchasing for seasonal demand

Online sales are often seasonal and promotion-driven. Reorder points, lead times and the planning worksheet help buyers order ahead of peaks rather than reacting to stockouts, using actual sales history from every channel rather than one platform’s report.

Reconciling payouts

Marketplace and payment payouts net together sales, refunds, fees and reserves. Bringing those transactions into Business Central with the orders lets finance match each payout to the bank and post fees to the right accounts, instead of reconciling in a spreadsheet each week.

Returns that update stock and finance together

Return orders receive goods back into stock (or into a quarantine location), credit the customer and adjust revenue and cost in one flow. You can see return rates by product and channel, which often explains margin problems that sales reports hide.

Bundles, kits and B2B

Bundles can be sold as one product and shipped as their components using assembly items. Brands that also sell wholesale can run B2B orders with customer price lists and payment terms in the same system as their online sales.

When to make the move

Common triggers are a second sales channel or warehouse, a 3PL, wholesale customers alongside online sales, or finance spending days each month reconciling payouts. If two or more of those apply, an ERP usually pays for itself in time saved and stock mistakes avoided.

Working with a 3PL

Many brands use a third-party logistics provider for some or all fulfillment. Business Central treats the 3PL as a location: stock is transferred to it, orders are sent to it for fulfillment, and shipment confirmations come back to post the shipment and invoice. The connection is usually made through EDI or the 3PL’s own API, and the 3PL’s stock reports are reconciled against Business Central regularly.

Getting the item and stock setup right in Business Central first makes every channel connection simpler afterwards.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Have a different question? Ask directly.

Is Business Central a good fit for an e-commerce brand?

Yes, once you have real inventory, more than one sales channel or a warehouse to run. Smaller stores selling a few products through one channel may be fine with simpler tools for now.

Can Business Central handle Amazon and other marketplaces?

Yes, through connector apps from Microsoft AppSource. Shopify has Microsoft’s own connector; other channels use specialist apps. Shopify integration →

Can we use a third-party warehouse?

Yes. Orders can be passed to a 3PL and shipment confirmations posted back, often over EDI or an API. EDI integration →
