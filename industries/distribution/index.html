Common challenges

Where distributors lose time and margin

  • Inventory you can’t trust. System counts drift from what’s on the shelf.
  • Manual purchasing. Buyers reorder from memory or spreadsheets.
  • Picking errors. Wrong items or quantities shipped, and costly returns.
  • Hidden costs. Freight and duty aren’t added to item costs, so margins look better than they are.

In depth

Running a distribution business on Business Central

Distributors live on thin margins and fast turns. The system has to tell you what you have, where it is, what it really cost, and what to buy next, without anyone rebuilding a spreadsheet.

Inventory you can trust

Business Central tracks stock by location and, where you use them, by bin. Every receipt, shipment, transfer and adjustment is a posted entry, so you can see exactly why a quantity changed. Cycle counting lets you count a few items or bins each day instead of shutting down for an annual count, and variances post straight to the general ledger.

Purchasing driven by demand

Each item can carry a reorder point, a safety stock, a lead time and a preferred vendor. The requisition worksheet looks at stock on hand, open sales orders and open purchase orders, then suggests what to order and when. Buyers review the suggestions and turn them into purchase orders in a few clicks, instead of guessing from memory.

Landed cost and true margin

For importers and anyone paying freight, duty or brokerage, item charges assign those costs to the items they belong to, by quantity, weight, volume or value. The item’s cost, and therefore your margin, then reflects what the goods actually cost to land in your warehouse.

Picking and shipping

Warehouse setup scales with your operation. A small team can ship straight from the sales order. A busier warehouse uses warehouse shipments and pick documents grouped by bin. High-volume operations add directed put-away and pick, and handheld scanning with a warehouse app such as Warehouse Insight.

Customer-specific pricing

Price lists handle customer-specific prices, price groups, quantity breaks and date ranges, and line discounts can be set by customer and item group. Sales staff see the right price as they enter the order, with stock availability and the customer’s credit limit checked on the same screen.

Selling online and through EDI

Many distributors sell through several channels at once: sales reps, a web store and large retail customers on EDI. Business Central can take orders from Shopify and EDI trading partners into the same order flow, so stock and margin are always measured across all channels.

Reporting distributors rely on

Margin by customer, item and salesperson; inventory turns and days on hand by location; slow-moving and dead stock; fill rate and backorders; and vendor performance on lead times. Most are available in Business Central and Excel from day one, and Power BI adds trend dashboards for leadership.

Lot, serial and expiration tracking

Distributors of food, chemicals, medical or electrical products often need to trace what went where. Business Central tracks lot and serial numbers from receipt to shipment, records expiration dates and can enforce first-expired, first-out picking, so a recall can be traced to the customers affected in minutes.

Drop shipments and special orders

For items you don’t stock, Business Central links the sales order to a purchase order so the vendor ships straight to your customer, or orders goods in specifically for one customer. Both keep margin and delivery visible on the original sales order.
