Common challenges

What holds growing manufacturers back

Most small manufacturers start with accounting software and spreadsheets. That works until order volume grows and nobody can say what’s in production, what’s short or what a product really costs.

  • No production visibility. Order status lives on the shop floor, not in the system.
  • Costs worked out in spreadsheets. Material and labor costs are estimates.
  • Material shortages. Components run out because demand isn’t planned.
  • Disconnected systems. Production, stock and accounting don’t share data.

How it flows

A production order, start to finish

  1. Plan

    The planning worksheet turns sales demand into suggested production and purchase orders.

  2. Release

    Released production orders reserve components and create pick work for the warehouse.

  3. Produce

    Consumption and output are recorded against each operation in the routing.

  4. Finish

    Finished goods move into stock, ready to ship.

  5. Cost

    Cost adjustment calculates actual costs and variances against standard.

Is it the right fit?

Where Business Central manufacturing works best

Business Central suits most small and midsize discrete manufacturers. A few situations need an add-on or a different product, and it’s better to know that before you start.

  • A good fit: make-to-stock and make-to-order production, assembly and kitting, and light fabrication with defined routings.
  • Needs an add-on: batch or recipe-based process manufacturing, advanced shop-floor scheduling, or detailed quality management.
  • Consider Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management: multi-site, high-volume production with complex planning across plants.
Worked example · Illustrative scenario

A fabrication shop moving production off spreadsheets

A 40-person metal fabrication company builds made-to-order products from about 600 components. Production is planned on a whiteboard, costs are estimated in a spreadsheet and the accountant only learns the real margin weeks after shipping.

StageWhat happens
DesignModel three-level bills of materials, routings across four work centers and standard costs for components and labor.
BuildSet up Premium manufacturing, production order statuses, and warehouse picks for releasing components to the floor.
DataLoad items, BOMs and routings from the existing spreadsheets after cleansing duplicates and missing units of measure.
TestRun sample orders end to end, from sales order to planning worksheet, production order, output, shipment and invoice.
Go-liveStart with one product family, then add the rest once the floor team is comfortable recording output.

Outcome: The company plans production from real demand, sees shortages before they stop the floor, and gets cost variances for every order instead of guessing at margins.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Have a different question? Ask directly.

Do we need Business Central Premium for manufacturing?

For production orders, routings, work centers and capacity planning, yes. Those features are only in the Premium license. Simple kitting with assembly orders is available in Essentials. Estimate your license cost →

Can Business Central handle process manufacturing?

Business Central is built for discrete manufacturing: products made from distinct parts. Batch, formula or recipe-based production, such as food or chemicals, usually needs a specialist add-on from Microsoft AppSource. We’ll tell you early if that applies to you.

How do shop-floor workers record production?

Through output and consumption journals or the production order pages, on a desktop, tablet or shop-floor terminal. Shared terminals can use a lower-cost device license instead of named user licenses.

Should we use standard or actual costing?

Many manufacturers use standard costing so they can see variances between planned and actual costs. Others prefer average or FIFO costing for simplicity. We’ll recommend a method based on how you price and report.
