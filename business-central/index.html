Choosing the right ERP

Is Business Central enough for your operations?

For most small and midsize distributors it is. For very high-volume or complex warehousing, an extension or Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management may be a better fit. Because we've worked hands-on with both, we can give you a straight answer.

Often the right fit

Business Central

Standard inventory and warehouse processes: receiving, put-away, picking, shipping and bin-level stock for small and midsize operations.

When you need more

Business Central plus a warehouse extension

Barcode scanning, handheld devices or more advanced picking, added through a specialist app from Microsoft AppSource.

For complex operations

Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management

High-volume or multi-site warehousing, advanced wave picking, transportation management and complex supply chains.

Moving from QuickBooks?

Signs you've outgrown entry-level accounting

  • Inventory lives in spreadsheets. Stock counts and costs are tracked outside your accounting system.
  • Several companies or locations. Consolidation and inter-company work take days.
  • Reporting is manual. Month-end means exporting and rebuilding reports in Excel.
  • Too many disconnected tools. Orders, stock and accounting are reconciled by hand.
In depth

How Business Central works day to day

Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central is a cloud ERP for small and midsize companies. It replaces separate accounting, inventory and order systems with one database, so a sales order, the stock it reserves, the shipment and the invoice are all the same record moving through stages.

Who Business Central is built for

It typically suits companies with roughly 10 to a few hundred employees, from distributors and manufacturers to professional services firms. Companies usually move to it from QuickBooks, Sage or older Microsoft products such as Dynamics GP and Dynamics NAV, when spreadsheets and disconnected tools start costing more than they save.

Role centers instead of menus

Each user opens Business Central to a role center: a home page built for their job. An accountant sees cash, receivables and payables; a warehouse manager sees open picks and shipments; a buyer sees items below their reorder point. Every number can be drilled into to see the entries behind it.

Microsoft 365 built in

Business Central is designed to work with the tools most companies already use. Sales staff can work with customers and quotes from Outlook, finance can open almost any list in Excel and publish changes back, and colleagues can share a live record in a Teams conversation. Reports can be built in Power BI on live data.

Updates without upgrade projects

In the cloud version, Microsoft releases two major updates a year plus monthly updates. Custom features are built as extensions that sit beside Microsoft’s code rather than changing it, which is why updates no longer mean the costly upgrade projects that older ERPs required.

Essentials or Premium?

Essentials covers finance, sales, purchasing, inventory, warehousing and projects. Premium adds manufacturing and service management. Light users who mainly view data and approve documents can use the low-cost Team Members license. See Essentials vs Premium pricing for current prices and a calculator.

Where it reaches its limits

Very high-volume warehouses, complex multi-site manufacturing, or global groups with heavy consolidation needs may be better served by Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management, Finance, or a specialist add-on. An honest partner will tell you which side of that line you are on before you commit.

What it connects to

Business Central has a built-in Shopify connector, standard integration with Dynamics 365 Sales through Dataverse, and open APIs for other systems. Thousands of apps on Microsoft AppSource add specialist features such as EDI, shipping, sales tax, scanning and payroll connections. See integrations for the most common ones.

Business Central is sold through Microsoft partners, who also implement and support it. Choosing a partner who knows your industry is as important as choosing the product.
