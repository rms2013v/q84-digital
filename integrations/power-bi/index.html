Reports our clients ask for most

  • Margin by customer, item and salesperson. Including landed costs and discounts.
  • Inventory health. Stock value, slow movers, stockouts and days on hand by location.

  • Sales trends. This year against last year, by region, channel and product line.
  • Job and project profitability. Budget against actual for service businesses.
Good reports start in Business Central

Why setup matters

Power BI can only slice data the way Business Central records it. Clean dimensions, item categories and customer groups make reports simple; messy ones make them impossible.

  • Dimensions. Department, location, channel or project recorded on every transaction.
  • Categories. Item categories and customer groups that match how you report.
  • Definitions. One agreed meaning for margin, revenue and on-time delivery.
In depth

Building a Business Central reporting setup that lasts

Start with five numbers

The most useful reporting projects start small. Ask the leadership team which five numbers they look at every week, how they want them sliced and how often they need them. Build those first, get them trusted, then expand. A dashboard with forty charts that nobody trusts is worse than five numbers everyone believes.

Where reports should live

  • Business Central pages and lists for operational, always-current views such as open orders, stock or overdue invoices.
  • Excel for ad hoc analysis, using Business Central’s built-in Excel views and financial reports.
  • Power BI for trends, comparisons across companies or periods, and dashboards for leadership.

Data refresh and access

Power BI reports usually refresh on a schedule, several times a day. Access is controlled in Power BI by workspace and report, and can follow Business Central permissions where needed. People who build or share reports typically need a Power BI Pro license, which some Microsoft 365 plans include.

Keeping definitions consistent

Agree one definition for each key measure, such as revenue (gross or net of returns), margin (with or without landed cost and freight) and on-time delivery. Write them down and build them once in the data model, so every report uses the same numbers.

Common reports by role

  • Owner or CEO: revenue, gross margin, cash and receivables trend, compared with last year and budget.
  • Finance: aged receivables and payables, cash forecast, expenses by department, and month-end checks.
  • Sales: sales by rep, customer and product line, open quotes, and customers buying less than last year.
  • Operations: stock value and turns, stockouts, slow movers, on-time shipping and backorders.

Ready-made reports first

Microsoft provides Power BI reports for Business Central covering finance, sales, purchasing, inventory, projects and manufacturing. Starting from these and adjusting them is usually much faster than building from scratch, and they keep up with Microsoft’s data model as Business Central updates.

Data quality comes first

Reports only show what Business Central records. If salespeople aren’t assigned to customers, items have no categories, or transactions miss their department dimension, the reports will have gaps. Part of every reporting project is checking those basics and, where needed, making fields mandatory so the data stays clean from then on.

Sharing reports safely

Decide early who should see what. Leadership dashboards with margins and salaries shouldn’t be shared as widely as operational reports. Power BI workspaces and row-level security let you show each team only the data it needs, for example restricting sales reps to their own customers.

FAQ

Power BI questions

Have a different question? Ask directly.

Do we need Power BI licenses?

To build and share reports, people usually need a Power BI Pro license, which some Microsoft 365 plans include. Viewing reports inside Business Central follows the same Power BI licensing rules. We confirm the right setup for your team.

How current is the data?

Reports refresh on a schedule, typically several times a day. For truly live numbers, Business Central’s own pages and Excel views are always up to date.

Can we report across several companies?

Yes. Power BI can combine data from several Business Central companies into one report, which is often simpler than consolidating in Business Central for management reporting.
