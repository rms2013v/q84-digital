Why it matters

Getting EDI right saves real money

Retailers charge back for late ship notices, wrong labels and mismatched invoices. Most chargebacks come from data that was right in one system and wrong in another.

  • Orders without retyping. EDI orders become sales orders automatically, with exceptions flagged.
  • Ship notices from real data. Built from what was actually picked and packed.
  • Compliant labels. Carton and pallet labels with the barcodes partners require.
  • Invoices that match. Prices and quantities checked against the original order.
Our process

How we set up EDI

  • Collect partner requirements. Each retailer’s documents, rules and label specs.
  • Choose the provider. An EDI network with a proven Business Central connector.
  • Map and configure. EDI fields mapped to Business Central customers, items and units of measure.
  • Test and certify. Test documents exchanged until the partner signs off.
  • Go live and monitor. Exceptions reviewed daily until everything runs clean.
In depth

What an EDI setup involves

Choosing an EDI provider

Most small and midsize companies use an EDI provider rather than building EDI themselves. The provider connects to your trading partners, translates their documents, keeps up with each retailer’s rules and supplies a connector app for Business Central. When comparing providers, ask whether they already work with your trading partners, how they price per partner and per document, and how their Business Central connector handles errors.

Trading partner requirements

Each retailer publishes its own EDI guide: which documents it requires, the fields it expects, how ship notices must describe cartons and pallets, and which label formats it accepts. These guides drive the setup. Missing a requirement usually shows up as a chargeback rather than an error message, so we go through them line by line before go-live.

Item and customer cross-references

Retailers order using their own item numbers, UPCs and store or distribution center codes. Business Central’s item references and ship-to addresses map those to your items and locations, so incoming orders land on the right products and the right destination without manual lookups.

Carton and pallet labels

Many retailers require GS1-128 shipping labels with a serial shipping container code (SSCC) that matches the ship notice. That means capturing which items went into which carton during packing, which is usually done with a warehouse scanning app or a small extension.

Costs to expect

EDI costs usually include a one-time setup fee per trading partner, a monthly subscription, and sometimes per-document or per-kilocharacter charges from the provider. Implementation work on the Business Central side is quoted separately.

Testing with trading partners

Most large retailers require a test phase before they accept live documents. You exchange test purchase orders, ship notices and invoices, and the retailer checks each one against its rules. Plan a few weeks for this per trading partner, and start with the partner that matters most to your business.

Before you start

Gather each trading partner’s EDI guide, label specifications and vendor compliance manual, plus a list of the documents they require. Having these in hand on day one shortens the project considerably.

Keep a simple log of chargebacks by reason after go-live; it shows exactly where the EDI or warehouse process needs tightening.

FAQ

EDI questions

Have a different question? Ask directly.

Does Business Central have EDI built in?

No. EDI is handled by a specialist EDI provider, usually through an app from Microsoft AppSource that connects their network to Business Central. We help you choose the provider and set up the connection.

Which EDI documents do we need?

It depends on your trading partners. Most retailers require at least purchase orders (850), ship notices (856) and invoices (810), and some add order acknowledgments (855) or inventory updates (846). Each partner has its own rules, so we start from their requirements.

How long does it take to add a trading partner?

Usually a few weeks, most of it spent on the partner’s testing and certification. Once the first partner is live, the next ones are faster.
