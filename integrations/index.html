Our approach

Configure first, customize only when it pays off

Every custom line of code is something to maintain. We check whether standard features or an existing app can do the job before we build anything.

  • Fit check. Standard setup, AppSource app or custom extension: we compare the options and their running costs with you.
  • Clear data flow. We agree which system owns each piece of data, how often it syncs and what happens when something fails.
  • Built for updates. Extensions follow Microsoft’s extension model, which keeps Business Central updates much smoother. We still test custom work against each major release.
  • Tested properly. Integrations are tested with realistic data volumes, duplicates and error cases, not just the happy path.
  • Documented. You get notes on what was built, how it works and how to monitor it.
Worked example · Illustrative scenario

Connecting a Shopify store to Business Central

An e-commerce distributor sells through Shopify and keeps its accounting in Business Central. Staff re-enter every web order, stock levels are updated by hand twice a day, and the store sometimes sells items that are out of stock.

StageWhat happens
DesignAgree that Business Central owns items, prices and stock, and Shopify owns web orders. Map product variants to item variants.
SetupConfigure Microsoft’s Shopify connector: item sync, stock sync by location, and order import with customer matching.
RulesOrders with unknown items or failed payments go to a review list instead of creating bad sales orders.
TestingRun test orders, refunds and partial shipments through the full flow, from Shopify order to shipment, invoice and payment.
Go-liveSwitch on scheduled sync and train staff to clear the exceptions list each morning.

Outcome: Web orders arrive in Business Central without retyping, the store shows stock that matches the warehouse, and staff only touch the orders that genuinely need attention.

This example shows how we approach this kind of project. It is not a client case study.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Have a different question? Ask directly.

Will integrations break when Business Central updates?

Microsoft releases two major updates a year. Integrations built on its standard APIs and extension model are designed to survive them, and we test custom work against each release preview so problems show up before your production system updates.

Do integrations run in real time?

It depends on the connection. Some sync within minutes, others run on a schedule such as every 15 minutes or nightly. We pick the timing based on what the business needs, such as near real-time stock for an online store and nightly for reporting.

Do we always need custom code?

No. We check for a standard connector or an AppSource app first, such as Microsoft’s Shopify connector. Custom development is only for gaps those options can’t cover.

Are there running costs?

Sometimes. Connector apps often have a monthly subscription, and some integrations use Azure services billed by Microsoft. We include these costs in the estimate so there are no surprises.

Tell us what you use today. We’ll suggest the simplest reliable way to make it work with Business Central.
