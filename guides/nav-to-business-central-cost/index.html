How much does moving from NAV to Business Central cost?

An honest look at what drives the price of leaving Dynamics NAV, how the two paths compare, and what most companies end up spending.

RangesDriversPathsSaving moneyFAQ
Book a free ERP assessment

The short answer: moving from Dynamics NAV to Business Central typically starts at $35,000 to $50,000CA$35,000 to CA$50,000 for implementation services, plus monthly Business Central licenses. A small, lightly customized NAV system sits at that starting point. Heavy customization, several companies or manufacturing push it higher. The single biggest factor is how much custom code your NAV system carries.

Timing matters too. NAV 2016 is already out of support, NAV 2017 loses extended support on January 11, 2027, and NAV 2018, the last version, on January 11, 2028.

Two paths

Upgrade or re-implement: the cost trade-off

Technical upgrade

Pays off when NAV is recent and clean

  • Keeps setup, history and familiar structure
  • Every customization still rewritten in AL
  • Several steps for older versions add cost
  • Carries old data problems forward
Re-implementation

Pays off when NAV is old or heavily modified

  • Standard features first, less custom code to maintain
  • Master data, open items and summary history migrated
  • A chance to fix processes and data
  • Lower running costs after go-live

Keeping costs down

How to spend less on leaving NAV

  • Start with an object analysis. Knowing what each customization does is the cheapest way to cut scope.
  • Retire aggressively. Reports, fields and code nobody uses shouldn’t move.
  • Prefer apps to custom code. Proven AppSource apps cost less to maintain than your own extensions.
  • Clean data first. Delete duplicates and inactive records in NAV before migration starts.
  • Phase it. Finance and inventory first; warehousing and integrations next.
FAQ

NAV upgrade cost questions

Have a different question? Ask directly.

Is a technical upgrade cheaper than a re-implementation?

Not always. An upgrade keeps your setup and history, but every NAV customization still has to be rewritten as an AL extension, and older versions need several upgrade steps. For older or heavily customized NAV systems, a fresh implementation that migrates master data, open items and summary history is often cheaper and leaves you with a cleaner system.

How many customizations is “a lot”?

It depends on what they do more than how many there are. A useful first step is an object analysis: we list every modified or custom object in your NAV database and sort each into keep, replace with standard, replace with an app, or retire. Many customizations turn out to be features Business Central now has as standard. AL development →

What do Business Central licenses cost compared to NAV?

NAV was usually bought once with a yearly enhancement plan. Business Central online is a monthly subscription per user: Essentials or Premium for full users and low-cost Team Members licenses for people who mostly look things up and approve. Hosting, backups and upgrades are included. Business Central licensing →

Can we spread the cost?

Yes. Many NAV customers go live first with finance, sales, purchasing and inventory, then add warehousing, integrations or reporting in a second phase. Phasing spreads the cost and lowers the risk of each go-live.
